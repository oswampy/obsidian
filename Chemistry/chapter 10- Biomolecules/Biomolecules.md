

## Introduction

**Biomolecules** are organic molecules produced by living organisms that are essential for life.

The major classes are:
- Carbohydrates
    
- Proteins
    
- Enzymes
    
- Vitamins
    
- Nucleic acids
    
- Hormones
    

---

## Carbohydrates

Carbohydrates are **polyhydroxy aldehydes or ketones**, or compounds which give such molecules on hydrolysis.

General representation:

$C_x(H_2O)_y$

Examples:

- Glucose → $C_6H_{12}O_6$
    
- Fructose → $C_6H_{12}O_6$
    
- Sucrose → $C_{12}H_{22}O_{11}$
    
- Starch → $(C_6H_{10}O_5)_n$
    

---

## Classification of Carbohydrates

### Monosaccharides

Cannot be hydrolysed further.

Examples:

- Glucose
    
- Fructose
    
- Ribose
    
- Galactose
    

### Oligosaccharides

Give 2–10 monosaccharide units on hydrolysis.

Examples:

- Sucrose
    
- Maltose
    
- Lactose
    

### Polysaccharides

Give a large number of monosaccharide units on hydrolysis.

Examples:

- Starch
    
- Cellulose
    
- Glycogen
    

---

## Classification Based on Carbonyl Group

### Aldoses

Contain an aldehyde group:

$-CHO$

Example: **Glucose**

### Ketoses

Contain a ketone group:

$>C=O$

Example: **Fructose**

---

# Glucose

Glucose is an **aldohexose**.

Molecular formula:

$C_6H_{12}O_6$

It is also called **D-(+)-glucose** or **dextrose**.

### Open-chain structure

```text
       CHO
        |
H — C — OH
        |
HO — C — H
        |
H — C — OH
        |
H — C — OH
        |
       CH2OH
```

Important features:

- Six carbon atoms
    
- Aldehyde group at C-1
    
- Four $-OH$ groups on C-2, C-3, C-4 and C-5
    
- Primary alcohol group at C-6
    
- Four chiral centres
    

Therefore, the maximum number of stereoisomers is:

$2^4=16$

---

# Preparation of Glucose

### From sucrose

$C_{12}H_{22}O_{11}+H_2O\xrightarrow{H^+}C_6H_{12}O_6+C_6H_{12}O_6$

Products:

**Glucose + Fructose**

### From starch

$(C_6H_{10}O_5)_n+nH_2O\xrightarrow[\text{heat}]{H^+}nC_6H_{12}O_6$

---

# Reactions of Glucose

## Reaction with HI

On prolonged heating with HI, glucose gives **n-hexane**:

$C_6H_{12}O_6\xrightarrow[\text{heat}]{HI}C_6H_{14}$

This proves that glucose has a **straight chain of six carbon atoms**.

---

## Reaction with Hydroxylamine

Glucose reacts with hydroxylamine to form an oxime:

$RCHO+NH_2OH\rightarrow RCH=NOH+H_2O$

This confirms the presence of a **carbonyl group**.

---

## Reaction with HCN

Glucose reacts with HCN to form a cyanohydrin:

$RCHO+HCN\rightarrow RCH(OH)CN$

This confirms the presence of a **carbonyl group**.

---

## Oxidation with Bromine Water

Glucose is oxidised by bromine water to gluconic acid:

$-CHO\xrightarrow{Br_2/H_2O}-COOH$

Therefore:

**Glucose → Gluconic acid**

This confirms the presence of an **aldehyde group**.

---

## Oxidation with Nitric Acid

Concentrated $HNO_3$ oxidises both terminal groups:

$CHO-(CHOH)_4-CH_2OH\xrightarrow{HNO_3}COOH-(CHOH)_4-COOH$

Product:

**Saccharic acid / Glucaric acid**

This proves the presence of:

- One aldehyde group
    
- One primary alcohol group
    

---

## Acetylation of Glucose

Glucose reacts with excess acetic anhydride:

$\text{Glucose}+5(CH_3CO)_2O\rightarrow\text{Glucose pentaacetate}+5CH_3COOH$

Formation of pentaacetate proves the presence of:

$5\ OH$

groups.

---

# Cyclic Structure of Glucose

Glucose exists mainly in cyclic form in solution.

The aldehyde group at C-1 reacts with the $OH$ group at C-5 to form a cyclic hemiacetal.

The resulting ring contains:

- Five carbon atoms
    
- One oxygen atom
    

Therefore, it is called a **pyranose ring**.

The cyclic structure creates a new chiral centre at C-1.

C-1 is called the:

**Anomeric carbon**

---

# $\alpha$ and $\beta$ Glucose

Two forms of cyclic glucose are possible.

### $\alpha$-D-glucose

The $OH$ group at the anomeric carbon is on the side opposite to the $CH_2OH$ group in the conventional Haworth representation.

### $\beta$-D-glucose

The $OH$ group at the anomeric carbon is on the same side as the $CH_2OH$ group.

These two forms are called:

**Anomers**

---

# Mutarotation

The gradual change in optical rotation when a pure $\alpha$ or $\beta$ form of a sugar is dissolved in water until equilibrium is reached is called **mutarotation**.

$\alpha\text{-D-glucose}\rightleftharpoons\text{open-chain glucose}\rightleftharpoons\beta\text{-D-glucose}$

Approximate equilibrium composition:

$\alpha:\beta\approx36:64$

Optical rotations:

$[\alpha]_D\approx+112^\circ$

$[\beta]_D\approx+19^\circ$

Equilibrium rotation:

$[\alpha]_D\approx+52.7^\circ$

---

# Why Glucose Gives Reducing Tests

Although glucose contains an aldehyde group in its open-chain form, in solution it exists predominantly in the cyclic hemiacetal form.

The cyclic form can open up and regenerate the aldehyde form:

$\text{Cyclic glucose}\rightleftharpoons\text{Open-chain glucose}$

Therefore glucose can reduce:

- Tollens' reagent
    
- Fehling's solution
    
- Alkaline $Cu^{2+}$ ions
    

Hence glucose is a:

**Reducing sugar**

---

# Fructose

Fructose is a:

**Ketohexose**

Molecular formula:

$C_6H_{12}O_6$

It contains a ketone group at C-2.

### Open-chain structure

```text
      CH2OH
        |
       C=O
        |
HO — C — H
        |
H — C — OH
        |
H — C — OH
        |
      CH2OH
```

---

# Cyclic Structure of Fructose

Fructose generally forms a five-membered ring.

This is called a:

**Furanose ring**

Cyclisation occurs between:

- C-2 carbonyl group
    
- C-5 hydroxyl group
    

Therefore, the anomeric carbon in fructose is:

$C_2$

---

# Why Fructose is a Reducing Sugar

Although fructose is a ketose, it gives Tollens' and Fehling's tests in alkaline medium.

It undergoes tautomerisation:

$\text{Fructose}\rightleftharpoons\text{Enediol}\rightleftharpoons\text{Glucose/Mannose}$

Glucose and mannose contain aldehyde groups and can reduce the reagents.

Therefore:

**Fructose is a reducing sugar.**

---

# Disaccharides

Disaccharides contain two monosaccharide units.

The monosaccharide units are joined through a:

**Glycosidic linkage**

Important disaccharides:

- Sucrose
    
- Maltose
    
- Lactose
    

---

# Sucrose

Sucrose is common table sugar.

Formula:

$C_{12}H_{22}O_{11}$

It consists of:

**Glucose + Fructose**

The linkage is:

$\alpha(1\rightarrow2)\beta$

The C-1 of $\alpha$-D-glucose is linked to C-2 of $\beta$-D-fructose.

### Hydrolysis

$C_{12}H_{22}O_{11}+H_2O\rightarrow C_6H_{12}O_6+C_6H_{12}O_6$

Therefore:

**Sucrose → Glucose + Fructose**

### Reducing nature

Sucrose is:

**Non-reducing**

Both anomeric carbons are involved in the glycosidic bond, so there is no free anomeric carbon.

---

# Invert Sugar

Hydrolysis of sucrose produces glucose and fructose:

$\text{Sucrose}\rightarrow\text{Glucose + Fructose}$

The optical rotation changes from positive to negative because of the strong negative rotation of fructose.

The hydrolysis product is called:

**Invert sugar**

---

# Maltose

Maltose consists of:

**Glucose + Glucose**

Linkage:

$\alpha(1\rightarrow4)$

The C-1 of one glucose is linked to C-4 of another glucose.

One anomeric carbon remains free.

Therefore:

**Maltose is a reducing sugar.**

---

# Lactose

Lactose consists of:

**Galactose + Glucose**

Linkage:

$\beta(1\rightarrow4)$

One anomeric carbon remains free.

Therefore:

**Lactose is a reducing sugar.**

Lactose is commonly called **milk sugar**.

---

# Comparison of Disaccharides

|Sugar|Components|Linkage|Reducing?|
|---|---|---|---|
|Sucrose|Glucose + Fructose|$\alpha(1\rightarrow2)\beta$|No|
|Maltose|Glucose + Glucose|$\alpha(1\rightarrow4)$|Yes|
|Lactose|Galactose + Glucose|$\beta(1\rightarrow4)$|Yes|

---

# Polysaccharides

Polysaccharides are high molecular mass carbohydrates consisting of many monosaccharide units.

Important examples:

- Starch
    
- Cellulose
    
- Glycogen
    

---

# Starch

Starch is the main storage carbohydrate of plants.

It consists of glucose units.

It has two components:

### Amylose

- Linear
    
- Unbranched
    
- $\alpha$-D-glucose units
    
- $\alpha(1\rightarrow4)$ linkage
    

### Amylopectin

- Branched
    
- $\alpha(1\rightarrow4)$ linkages in chains
    
- $\alpha(1\rightarrow6)$ linkages at branch points
    

### Hydrolysis

$\text{Starch}\xrightarrow{H_2O/H^+}\text{Glucose}$

---

# Cellulose

Cellulose is the main structural component of plant cell walls.

It consists of:

$\beta$-D-glucose units.

Linkage:

$\beta(1\rightarrow4)$

It is:

- Linear
    
- Unbranched
    
- Structural
    

---

# Why Humans Cannot Digest Cellulose

Humans lack the enzyme:

**Cellulase**

Therefore, humans cannot efficiently hydrolyse the $\beta(1\rightarrow4)$ glycosidic bonds of cellulose.

---

# Glycogen

Glycogen is the storage carbohydrate of:

- Animals
    
- Fungi
    

In humans it is stored mainly in:

- Liver
    
- Muscles
    

It is **highly branched**.

This allows rapid release of glucose when required.

---

# Starch vs Cellulose vs Glycogen

|Property|Starch|Cellulose|Glycogen|
|---|---|---|---|
|Main role|Plant storage|Plant structure|Animal storage|
|Monomer|Glucose|Glucose|Glucose|
|Main linkage|$\alpha(1\rightarrow4)$|$\beta(1\rightarrow4)$|$\alpha(1\rightarrow4)$|
|Branching|Amylose unbranched, amylopectin branched|None|Highly branched|
|Digested by humans|Yes|No|Yes|

---

# Proteins

Proteins are polymers of **$\alpha$-amino acids** linked together by peptide bonds.

They are essential for:

- Growth
    
- Repair
    
- Enzymes
    
- Hormones
    
- Antibodies
    
- Muscles
    
- Transport
    

---

# $\alpha$-Amino Acids

General structure:

$NH_2-CH(R)-COOH$

The amino group and carboxyl group are attached to the same carbon.

That carbon is called the:

**$\alpha$-carbon**

Example: Glycine

$NH_2-CH_2-COOH$

Example: Alanine

$CH_3-CH(NH_2)-COOH$

---

# Classification of Amino Acids

### Neutral amino acids

Contain one $NH_2$ and one $COOH$ group.

Examples:

- Glycine
    
- Alanine
    

### Acidic amino acids

Contain additional $COOH$ groups.

Examples:

- Aspartic acid
    
- Glutamic acid
    

### Basic amino acids

Contain additional amino groups or basic groups.

Examples:

- Lysine
    
- Arginine
    

---

# Essential and Non-essential Amino Acids

### Essential amino acids

Cannot be synthesised sufficiently by the human body and therefore must be obtained from the diet.

Examples:

- Valine
    
- Leucine
    
- Isoleucine
    
- Lysine
    
- Methionine
    
- Threonine
    
- Tryptophan
    
- Phenylalanine
    

### Non-essential amino acids

Can be synthesised by the body.

Examples:

- Glycine
    
- Alanine
    
- Glutamic acid
    

---

# Zwitterion

Amino acids contain both acidic $-COOH$ and basic $-NH_2$ groups.

A proton transfer occurs:

$NH_2-CHR-COOH\rightleftharpoons{}^+NH_3-CHR-COO^-$

The resulting ion is called a:

**Zwitterion**

It contains both:

- Positive charge
    
- Negative charge
    

but has:

**Zero net charge**

---

# Isoelectric Point

The **isoelectric point ($pI$)** is the pH at which an amino acid has zero net charge and exists predominantly as a zwitterion.

At:

$pH=pI$

the amino acid does not migrate towards either electrode in an electric field.

---

# Acid-Base Behaviour of Amino Acids

### In acidic medium

$^+NH_3-CHR-COOH$

Net charge:

$+1$

### At isoelectric point

$^+NH_3-CHR-COO^-$

Net charge:

$0$

### In basic medium

$NH_2-CHR-COO^-$

Net charge:

$-1$

---

# Peptide Bond

Two amino acids undergo condensation to form a peptide bond.

$NH_2CHRCOOH+NH_2CHR'COOH\rightarrow NH_2CHRCONHCHR'COOH+H_2O$

The bond:

$-CO-NH-$

is called the:

**Peptide bond**

---

# Dipeptide

Two amino acids form a dipeptide.

Example:

$\text{Glycine + Alanine}\rightarrow\text{Glycylalanine}+H_2O$

A dipeptide contains:

**One peptide bond**

---

# Tripeptide

Three amino acids form a tripeptide.

A tripeptide contains:

**Two peptide bonds**

---

# Polypeptide

A large number of amino acids linked through peptide bonds form a:

**Polypeptide**

Proteins generally consist of one or more polypeptide chains.

---

# Structure of Proteins

Protein structure is described at four levels:

- Primary structure
    
- Secondary structure
    
- Tertiary structure
    
- Quaternary structure
    

---

# Primary Structure

The exact sequence of amino acids in a polypeptide chain is called its:

**Primary structure**

Example:

$Gly-Ala-Val-Leu-\cdots$

---

# Secondary Structure

The regular folding of the polypeptide chain due mainly to hydrogen bonding is called secondary structure.

The two major forms are:

### $\alpha$-Helix

- Spiral structure
    
- Stabilised mainly by hydrogen bonds
    

### $\beta$-Pleated Sheet

- Extended chain arrangement
    
- Stabilised by hydrogen bonds
    

---

# Tertiary Structure

The overall three-dimensional arrangement of a single polypeptide chain is called its:

**Tertiary structure**

It is stabilised by:

- Hydrogen bonds
    
- Ionic interactions
    
- Disulfide bonds
    
- van der Waals forces
    
- Hydrophobic interactions
    

---

# Quaternary Structure

When a protein contains more than one polypeptide chain, the arrangement of these chains is called:

**Quaternary structure**

Example:

**Haemoglobin**

---

# Denaturation of Proteins

Loss of biological activity due to disruption of the higher-order structure of a protein is called:

**Denaturation**

Causes include:

- Heat
    
- Change in pH
    
- Certain chemicals
    

The **primary structure generally remains intact**.

Example:

**Boiling of egg**

---

# Fibrous and Globular Proteins

|Fibrous proteins|Globular proteins|
|---|---|
|Long/thread-like|Compact/spherical|
|Generally insoluble|Generally soluble|
|Mainly structural|Mainly functional|
|Keratin|Insulin|

# Enzymes

Enzymes are mostly **globular proteins** that act as biological catalysts.

They:

- Increase reaction rate
    
- Are highly specific
    
- Work under mild conditions
    
- Are not consumed during the reaction
    

Example:

$\text{Starch}\xrightarrow{\text{amylase}}\text{Maltose}$

---

# Enzyme-Substrate Complex

The enzyme contains an **active site** where the substrate binds.

$E+S\rightleftharpoons ES\rightarrow E+P$

where:

- $E$ = Enzyme
    
- $S$ = Substrate
    
- $ES$ = Enzyme-substrate complex
    
- $P$ = Product
    

---

# Lock and Key Model

According to the lock-and-key model:

- Enzyme = Lock
    
- Substrate = Key
    

The active site has a shape complementary to the substrate.

Therefore enzymes show:

**High specificity**

---

# Factors Affecting Enzyme Activity

### Temperature

Increasing temperature generally increases enzyme activity initially.

Beyond the optimum temperature:

**Denaturation occurs**

and activity decreases.

### pH

Each enzyme has an optimum pH.

### Substrate concentration

Initially, increasing substrate concentration increases the reaction rate.

At high substrate concentration, enzyme active sites become saturated and the rate approaches a maximum.

# Nucleic Acids

Nucleic acids are polymers of **nucleotides**.

The two major types are:

**DNA — Deoxyribonucleic acid**

**RNA — Ribonucleic acid**

---

# Nucleoside vs Nucleotide

### Nucleoside

$Sugar+Nitrogenous\ base$

### Nucleotide

$Sugar+Nitrogenous\ base+Phosphate$

Therefore:

$Nucleotide=Nucleoside+Phosphate$

---

# Components of a Nucleotide

A nucleotide contains:

### Pentose sugar

- Ribose
    
- Deoxyribose
    

### Nitrogenous base

- Purine
    
- Pyrimidine
    

### Phosphate group

---

# Nitrogenous Bases

## Purines

**Adenine (A)** and **Guanine (G)**

Memory:

**Pure As Gold**

$A,G=\text{Purines}$

## Pyrimidines

- Cytosine (C)
    
- Thymine (T)
    
- Uracil (U)
    

---

# DNA

DNA contains:

### Sugar

**2-Deoxyribose**

### Bases

$A,G,C,T$

Therefore:

$DNA=A+G+C+T$

---

# RNA

RNA contains:

### Sugar

**Ribose**

### Bases

$A,G,C,U$

Therefore:

$RNA=A+G+C+U$

The major base difference is:

$DNA:\ T$

$RNA:\ U$

---

# Structure of DNA

DNA consists of two polynucleotide strands arranged in a:

**Double helix**

The two strands are:

**Antiparallel**

One strand runs:

$5'\rightarrow3'$

while the other runs:

$3'\rightarrow5'$

---

# Base Pairing in DNA

### Adenine-Thymine

$A=T$

They are connected by **2 hydrogen bonds**.

### Guanine-Cytosine

$G\equiv C$

They are connected by **3 hydrogen bonds**.

Therefore:

$A-T=2\ H\text{-bonds}$

$G-C=3\ H\text{-bonds}$

---

# DNA vs RNA

|Property|DNA|RNA|
|---|---|---|
|Sugar|Deoxyribose|Ribose|
|Pyrimidines|C, T|C, U|
|Usually|Double-stranded|Single-stranded|
|Main role|Stores genetic information|Protein synthesis and other functions|
|Stability|More stable|Less stable|

---

# DNA Replication

DNA can produce an identical copy of itself.

During replication:

- DNA double helix unwinds
    
- Hydrogen bonds between bases break
    
- Each strand acts as a template
    
- Complementary nucleotides are added
    
- Two DNA molecules are produced
    

This is called:

**Semiconservative replication**

Each new DNA molecule contains:

**One old strand + one new strand**

---

# Central Dogma

The flow of genetic information is:

$DNA\rightarrow RNA\rightarrow Protein$

---

# Transcription

The genetic information in DNA is copied into RNA.

$DNA\rightarrow RNA$

This process is called:

**Transcription**

---

# Translation

The information in mRNA is used to synthesise a protein.

$RNA\rightarrow Protein$

This process is called:

**Translation**

# Hormones

Hormones are chemical messengers secreted by endocrine glands.

They regulate various physiological processes.

Examples:

- Insulin
    
- Glucagon
    
- Adrenaline
    
- Thyroxine
    
- Testosterone
    
- Estrogen
    
- Progesterone
    

---

# Insulin

Insulin is a **peptide/protein hormone**.

It is secreted by the:

**Pancreas**

Its major function is to:

**Lower blood glucose**

---

# Glucagon

Glucagon is a peptide hormone secreted by the pancreas.

Its major effect is to:

**Increase blood glucose**

Therefore insulin and glucagon generally have opposite effects on blood glucose.

---

# Adrenaline

Adrenaline is secreted by the adrenal glands.

It is associated with the:

**Fight-or-flight response**

It increases:

- Heart rate
    
- Blood flow to muscles
    
- Blood glucose availability
    

---

# Thyroxine

Thyroxine is secreted by the:

**Thyroid gland**

It regulates:

**Metabolism**

Its synthesis requires iodine.

---

# Sex Hormones

### Testosterone

Male sex hormone.

### Estrogen

Female sex hormone.

### Progesterone

Important in the female reproductive cycle and pregnancy.

# High-Yield Definitions

### Glycosidic Linkage

The linkage formed between two monosaccharide units through an oxygen atom is called a **glycosidic linkage**.

### Peptide Bond

The bond:

$-CO-NH-$

linking two amino acids is called a **peptide bond**.

### Zwitterion

A molecule containing both positive and negative charges but having zero net charge.

### Denaturation

Loss of biological activity due to disruption of the higher-order structure of a protein.

### Enzyme

A biological catalyst, mostly proteinaceous in nature.

### Nucleoside

$Sugar+Nitrogenous\ base$

### Nucleotide

$Sugar+Nitrogenous\ base+Phosphate$

### Mutarotation

Change in optical rotation due to interconversion of $\alpha$ and $\beta$ forms through the open-chain form.

---

# Reducing vs Non-Reducing Sugars

### Reducing sugars

Have a free anomeric carbon or can generate a free carbonyl group.

Examples:

- Glucose
    
- Fructose
    
- Maltose
    
- Lactose
    

### Non-reducing sugar

Does not have a free anomeric carbon.

Example:

**Sucrose**
