
# Structure and Reactions of Amines

## Physical Properties of Amines

- ### Boiling Points
    
    - **Intermolecular Hydrogen Bonding:** Primary ($1^\circ$) and secondary ($2^\circ$) amines undergo intermolecular hydrogen bonding due to the presence of polar $\text{N-H}$ bonds. Primary amines form more hydrogen bonds per molecule than secondary amines. Tertiary ($3^\circ$) amines lack $\text{N-H}$ bonds and cannot form intermolecular hydrogen bonds among themselves (only dipole-dipole interactions).
        
    - **Order of Boiling Points:**
        
        $$\text{Primary } (1^\circ) > \text{Secondary } (2^\circ) > \text{Tertiary } (3^\circ)$$
        
    - **Comparison with Alcohols:** Alcohols have higher boiling points than amines of comparable molar mass because oxygen is more electronegative than nitrogen ($\text{O } 3.5\text{ vs. N } 3.0$), making $\text{O-H}\cdots\text{O}$ hydrogen bonds significantly stronger than $\text{N-H}\cdots\text{N}$ bonds.
        
- ### Solubility
    
    - Lower aliphatic amines form hydrogen bonds with water molecules and are miscible in water.
        
    - Solubility decreases with an increase in the size of the hydrophobic alkyl chain ($\text{R}-$ group).
        
    - Aniline and other aromatic amines are practically insoluble in water due to the large hydrophobic aryl ring overwhelming the polar $-\text{NH}_2$ group.
        

# General Methods of Preparation (MOP)

- ## 1. Reduction of Nitro Compounds
    
    - **Reagents:** * $\text{H}_2 / \text{Pd}$ or $\text{Pt}$ in ethanol
        
        - $\text{Fe} + \text{HCl}$ or $\text{Sn} + \text{HCl}$
            
    - **Preferred Method:** $\text{Fe} + \text{HCl}$ is preferred industrially because $\text{FeCl}_2$ formed during the reaction hydrolyzes to regenerate $\text{HCl}$:
        
        $$\text{FeCl}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow \text{Fe(OH)}_2 + 2\text{HCl}$$
        
        Thus, only a catalytic amount of hydrochloric acid is required to initiate the reaction.
        
    - **General Reaction:**
        
        $$\text{R-NO}_2 + 6[\text{H}] \xrightarrow{\text{Fe / HCl}} \text{R-NH}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$$
        
- ## 2. Ammonolysis of Alkyl Halides (Hofmann’s Method)
    
    - **Nucleophilic Substitution:** The $\text{C-X}$ bond in alkyl halides is cleaved by a nucleophilic attack of ammonia ($\text{NH}_3$) at $373\text{ K}$ in a sealed tube.
        
    - **Product Mixture:** The primary amine formed acts as a nucleophile and further reacts with excess alkyl halide, yielding a mixture of $1^\circ, 2^\circ, 3^\circ$ amines and quaternary ammonium salts ($\text{R}_4\text{N}^+\text{X}^-$).
        
        $$\text{NH}_3 \xrightarrow[\text{-HX}]{\text{R-X}} \text{R-NH}_2 \xrightarrow[\text{-HX}]{\text{R-X}} \text{R}_2\text{NH} \xrightarrow[\text{-HX}]{\text{R-X}} \text{R}_3\text{N} \xrightarrow{\text{R-X}} \text{R}_4\text{N}^+\text{X}^-$$
        
    - **Controlled Synthesis:** Primary amine is obtained as the **major product** if excess ammonia ($\text{NH}_3$) is used.
        
- ## 3. Reduction of Nitriles & Amides
    
    - **Reduction of Nitriles ($\text{R-CN}$):** Reduced using $\text{LiAlH}_4$ or catalytic hydrogenation ($\text{H}_2/\text{Ni}$ or $\text{Na(Hg)} / \text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH}$) to yield primary amines with the **same number of carbon atoms**.
        
        $$\text{R-C}\equiv\text{N} \xrightarrow{\text{LiAlH}_4 \text{ or } \text{H}_2/\text{Ni}} \text{R-CH}_2\text{-NH}_2$$
        
    - **Reduction of Amides ($\text{R-CONH}_2$):** Reduced exclusively by $\text{LiAlH}_4 / \text{H}_2\text{O}$ to primary amines without carbon loss.
        
        $$\text{R-CONH}_2 \xrightarrow[2.\, \text{H}_2\text{O}]{1.\, \text{LiAlH}_4} \text{R-CH}_2\text{-NH}_2$$
        
- ## 4. Gabriel Phthalimide Synthesis
    
    - **Specific Use:** Used for the exclusive preparation of **pure aliphatic primary amines**.
        
    - **Mechanism Steps:**
        
        1. **Deprotonation:** Phthalimide reacts with ethanolic $\text{KOH}$ to remove the acidic imide proton, forming potassium phthalimide.
            
        2. **$\text{S}_\text{N}2$ Alkylation:** The nitrogen anion attacks an alkyl halide ($\text{R-X}$) via an $\text{S}_\text{N}2$ mechanism to form $N$-alkylphthalimide.
            
        3. **Hydrolysis:** Alkaline hydrolysis ($\text{NaOH / H}_2\text{O}$) cleaves the two amide bonds to yield primary amine ($\text{R-NH}_2$) and potassium phthalate.
            
    - **Detailed Mechanism:**
        
        $$\text{Phthalimide} \xrightarrow{\text{KOH}} \text{Potassium Phthalimide} \xrightarrow{\text{R-X} (\text{S}_\text{N}2)} N\text{-Alkylphthalimide} \xrightarrow{\text{NaOH (aq)}} \text{R-NH}_2 + \text{Phthalate Salt}$$
        
    - **Limitation:** Aromatic primary amines **cannot** be prepared by this method because aryl halides do not undergo nucleophilic substitution ($\text{S}_\text{N}2$) with the phthalimide anion due to partial double bond character ($\text{C-X}$) and steric hindrance from the $\pi$-electron cloud.
        
- ## 5. Hofmann Bromamide Degradation Reaction
    
    - **General Pattern:** Conversion of a primary amide to a primary amine containing **one carbon atom less** than the parent amide.
        
    - **Reagents:** Amide treated with bromine ($\text{Br}_2$) in an aqueous or ethanolic solution of sodium hydroxide ($\text{NaOH}$).
        
    - **General Equation:**
        
        $$\text{R-CONH}_2 + \text{Br}_2 + 4\text{NaOH} \rightarrow \text{R-NH}_2 + \text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3 + 2\text{NaBr} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$$
        
    - **Reaction Mechanism:**
        
        1. Base deprotonates the amide to form an enolate/amide anion.
            
        2. Nitrogen attacks $\text{Br}_2$ to form $N$-bromoamide:
            
            $$\text{R-CONH}_2 + \text{OH}^- + \text{Br}_2 \rightarrow \text{R-CONH-Br} + \text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{Br}^-$$
            
        3. Base removes second proton from $N$-bromoamide to give an acyl nitrene intermediate (or concerted transition state).
            
        4. **Alkyl Group Migration:** The alkyl group ($\text{R}-$) migrates from carbonyl carbon to nitrogen, forming an **isocyanate intermediate ($\text{R-N=C=O}$)**.
            
        5. **Hydrolysis:** Isocyanate is hydrolyzed by base to give an unstable carbamic acid ($\text{R-NHCOOH}$), which spontaneously decarboxylates to yield the primary amine ($\text{R-NH}_2$) and carbonate ($\text{CO}_3^{2-}$).
            

# Basicity & Chemical Reactions of Amines

- ### Basicity Trends
    
    - **Gas Phase Order:** Basicity depends purely on the electron-donating inductive (+I) effect of alkyl groups:
        
        $$\text{Tertiary } (3^\circ) > \text{Secondary } (2^\circ) > \text{Primary } (1^\circ) > \text{NH}_3$$
        
    - **Aqueous Phase Order:** Controlled by three competing factors: **+I effect**, **solvation energy of the conjugate ammonium cation**, and **steric hindrance**:
        
        - **Methyl Substituents ($-\text{CH}_3$):**
            
            $$(2^\circ > 1^\circ > 3^\circ > \text{NH}_3) \implies (\text{CH}_3)_2\text{NH} > \text{CH}_3\text{NH}_2 > (\text{CH}_3)_3\text{N} > \text{NH}_3$$
            
        - **Ethyl Substituents ($-\text{C}_2\text{H}_5$):**
            
            $$(2^\circ > 3^\circ > 1^\circ > \text{NH}_3) \implies (\text{C}_2\text{H}_5)_2\text{NH} > (\text{C}_2\text{H}_5)_3\text{N} > \text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2 > \text{NH}_3$$
            
    - **Aromatic vs. Aliphatic Amines:** Arylamines (e.g., Aniline) are significantly **less basic** than aliphatic amines because:
        
        1. The unshared electron pair on nitrogen is delocalized over the benzene ring via resonance (5 resonance structures).
            
        2. Anilinium ion formed after protonation has only 2 resonance structures, making aniline more stable than its protonated form.
            
- ### Key Chemical Tests & Reactions
    
    - **1. Acylation & Benzoylation (Schotten-Baumann Reaction)**
        
        - $1^\circ$ and $2^\circ$ aliphatic/aromatic amines undergo nucleophilic substitution with acid chlorides or anhydrides in the presence of a base stronger than the amine (e.g., pyridine) to remove $\text{HCl}$.
            
        - Reaction of aniline with benzoyl chloride in aqueous $\text{NaOH}$ is called the **Schotten-Baumann reaction**:
            
            $$\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2 + \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{COCl} \xrightarrow{\text{NaOH (aq)}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NHCOC}_6\text{H}_5 \quad (N\text{-Phenylbenzamide}) + \text{HCl}$$
            
    - **2. Carbylamine Reaction (Isocyanide Test)**
        
        - **Specific To:** Aliphatic and aromatic **primary ($1^\circ$) amines only**.
            
        - **Reagents:** Chloroform ($\text{CHCl}_3$) and ethanolic $\text{KOH}$ with heat.
            
        - **Observation:** Formation of an extremely foul-smelling alkyl/aryl isocyanide (carbylamine).
            
            $$\text{R-NH}_2 + \text{CHCl}_3 + 3\text{KOH} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{R-NC} + 3\text{KCl} + 3\text{H}_2\text{O}$$
            
        - **Mechanism:**
            
            1. Base deprotonates chloroform to generate a trichloromethyl anion ($:\text{CCl}_3^-$), which loses $\text{Cl}^-$ to form **dichlorocarbene ($:\text{CCl}_2$)** as an electrophilic reactive intermediate.
                
            2. Primary amine nucleophilically attacks the electrophilic dichlorocarbene.
                
            3. Subsequent elimination of two molecules of $\text{HCl}$ yields the isocyanide ($\text{R-N}\equiv\text{C}$).
                
    - **3. Reaction with Nitrous Acid ($\text{HNO}_2$)**
        
        - **Primary Aliphatic Amines:** React with $\text{HNO}_2$ ($\text{NaNO}_2 + \text{HCl}$) to form unstable aliphatic diazonium salts, which break down spontaneously to yield **$\text{N}_2$ gas quantitatively** (measured in analytical estimation of amino acids) and a mixture of alcohols, alkyl halides, and alkenes.
            
        - **Primary Aromatic Amines:** React at $273\text{--}278\text{ K}$ ($0\text{--}5^\circ\text{C}$) to give stable **arene diazonium salts**.
            
    - **4. Reaction with Arylsulphonyl Chloride (Hinsberg’s Test)**
        
        - Uses **Benzenesulphonyl chloride** ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{SO}_2\text{Cl}$) to distinguish between $1^\circ, 2^\circ$, and $3^\circ$ amines.
            
        - **$1^\circ$ Amine:** Reacts to form $N$-alkylbenzenesulphonamide. The hydrogen attached to nitrogen is strongly acidic due to the electron-withdrawing $-\text{SO}_2-$ group $\implies$ **Soluble in aqueous $\text{KOH}$**.
            
        - **$2^\circ$ Amine:** Reacts to form $N,N$-dialkylbenzenesulphonamide. Lacks an acidic hydrogen on nitrogen $\implies$ **Insoluble in aqueous $\text{KOH}$**.
            
        - **$3^\circ$ Amine:** Does not react due to the absence of acidic hydrogen on nitrogen.
            
- ### Electrophilic Substitution Reactions of Aniline
    
    - **Bromination:**
        
        - _Monobromination Control:_ Aniline is acetylated first with acetic anhydride/pyridine to form acetanilide. The lone pair on nitrogen is delocalized by the adjacent carbonyl group ($\text{-C=O}$), reducing the activating power of the $-\text{NH}_2$ group. Subsequent bromination yields **$p$-bromoaniline** as the major product after acidic/alkaline hydrolysis.
            
        
        $$\text{Aniline} \xrightarrow{\text{(CH}_3\text{CO)}_2\text{O / Pyridine}} \text{Acetanilide} \xrightarrow{\text{Br}_2 / \text{CH}_3\text{COOH}} p\text{-Bromoacetanilide} \xrightarrow{\text{H}^+ / \text{H}_2\text{O}} p\text{-Bromoaniline}$$
        
    - **Nitration:**
        
        - Direct nitration with conc. $\text{HNO}_3 / \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ yields:
            
            - **para (51%)**
                
            - **meta (47%)**
                
            - **ortho (2%)**
                
        - _Explanation for Meta Product:_ In strongly acidic nitration media, aniline gets protonated to form the **anilinium ion** ($\text{-NH}_3^+$), which is a strong electron-withdrawing group and acts as a **meta-director**.
            
    - **Sulphonation:**
        
        - Aniline reacts with conc. $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ to form anilinium hydrogen sulphate, which when heated at $453\text{--}473\text{ K}$ rearranges to **sulphanilic acid**.
            
        - Sulphanilic acid exists primarily as a dipolar ion called a **Zwitterion** ($\text{H}_3\text{N}^+-\text{C}_6\text{H}_4-\text{SO}_3^-$).
            

## Diazonium Salts ($\text{ArN}_2^+\text{X}^-$)

- ### Preparation (Diazotisation)
    
    - Prepared by treating primary aromatic amines with nitrous acid (generated _in situ_ from $\text{NaNO}_2 + \text{HCl}$) at $273\text{--}278\text{ K}$ ($0\text{--}5^\circ\text{C}$).
        
        $$\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2 + \text{NaNO}_2 + 2\text{HCl} \xrightarrow{273\text{--}278\text{ K}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^- + \text{NaCl} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$$
        
    - **Mechanism of Diazotisation:**
        
        1. Protonation of nitrous acid ($\text{HONO}$) yields the **nitrosonium cation ($:\text{N=O}^+$)** as the electrophile:
            
            $$\text{HONO} + \text{H}^+ \rightarrow \text{H}_2\text{O}^+-\text{NO} \rightarrow \text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{NO}^+$$
            
        2. Primary aromatic amine nucleophilically attacks $\text{NO}^+$ to form an $N$-nitrosamine intermediate.
            
        3. Proton transfers and loss of water yield the stable diazonium cation ($\text{Ar-N}\equiv\text{N}^+$).
            
- ### Chemical Reactions
    
    - **1. Reactions Involving Displacement of Nitrogen:**
        
        - **Sandmeyer Reaction:** Displacement of nitrogen by $-\text{Cl}$, $-\text{Br}$, or $-\text{CN}$ using **cuprous salts ($\text{Cu}_2\text{Cl}_2, \text{Cu}_2\text{Br}_2, \text{Cu}_2(\text{CN})_2$)** dissolved in corresponding halogen acids.
            
            $$\text{ArN}_2^+\text{Cl}^- \xrightarrow{\text{Cu}_2\text{Cl}_2 / \text{HCl}} \text{Ar-Cl} + \text{N}_2$$
            
        - **Gattermann Reaction:** Alternative method using **copper powder ($\text{Cu}$)** and corresponding halogen acid ($\text{HCl}$ or $\text{HBr}$).
            
            $$\text{ArN}_2^+\text{Cl}^- \xrightarrow{\text{Cu / HCl}} \text{Ar-Cl} + \text{N}_2 + \text{CuCl}$$
            
        - **Replacement by Iodide ($-\text{I}$):** Warm diazonium salt solution with potassium iodide ($\text{KI}$).
            
            $$\text{ArN}_2^+\text{Cl}^- + \text{KI} \rightarrow \text{Ar-I} + \text{KCl} + \text{N}_2$$
            
        - **Replacement by Fluoride ($-\text{F}$) (Schiemann Reaction):** Treat with fluoroboric acid ($\text{HBF}_4$) to precipitate $\text{ArN}_2^+\text{BF}_4^-$, then heat dry salt to yield aryl fluoride.
            
        - **Replacement by Hydroxyl ($-\text{OH}$):** Warm diazonium salt solution up to $283\text{ K}$ with dilute acid ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$).
            
            $$\text{ArN}_2^+\text{Cl}^- + \text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{Ar-OH} + \text{N}_2 + \text{HCl}$$
            
        - **Replacement by Hydrogen ($-\text{H}$):** Reduce using hypophosphorous acid ($\text{H}_3\text{PO}_2$) or ethanol ($\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{OH}$).
            
            $$\text{ArN}_2^+\text{Cl}^- + \text{H}_3\text{PO}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow \text{Ar-H} + \text{N}_2 + \text{H}_3\text{PO}_3 + \text{HCl}$$
            
    - **2. Reactions Involving Retention of Diazo Group (Coupling Reactions):**
        
        - **Phenol Coupling:** Reacts with phenol in a mildly basic medium ($\text{pH } 9\text{--}10$) to form an azo compound:
            
            $$\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^- + \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{OH} \xrightarrow{\text{OH}^-, \, \text{pH } 9-10} p\text{-hydroxyazobenzene (Orange Dye)} + \text{Cl}^- + \text{H}_2\text{O}$$
            
        - **Aniline Coupling:** Reacts with aniline in a mildly acidic medium ($\text{pH } 4\text{--}5$) to form an azo compound:
            
            $$\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2^+\text{Cl}^- + \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NH}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{H}^+, \, \text{pH } 4-5} p\text{-aminoazobenzene (Yellow Dye)} + \text{HCl}$$
