## 5. Isomerism in Coordination Compounds

### 5.1 What Is Isomerism?

Isomerism is the phenomenon in which compounds have the same molecular formula but different arrangements of atoms or ligands.

Coordination compounds show two major types of isomerism:

1. Structural isomerism.
    
2. Stereoisomerism.
    

### 5.2 Structural Isomerism

Structural isomers differ in the connectivity of atoms or the way ligands are attached.

Types:

1. Ionisation isomerism.
    
2. Hydrate or solvate isomerism.
    
3. Linkage isomerism.
    
4. Coordination isomerism.
    

### 5.3 Ionisation Isomerism

Ionisation isomerism occurs when an ion inside the coordination sphere exchanges places with an ion outside the coordination sphere.

Example:

$[Co(NH_3)_5Br]SO_4$

and

$[Co(NH_3)_5SO_4]Br$

The first produces sulphate ions in solution, whereas the second produces bromide ions.

### 5.4 Hydrate Isomerism

Hydrate isomerism occurs when water molecules inside and outside the coordination sphere exchange positions.

Example:

$[Cr(H_2O)_6]Cl_3$

$[Cr(H_2O)_5Cl]Cl_2\cdot H_2O$

$[Cr(H_2O)_4Cl_2]Cl\cdot2H_2O$

These compounds contain the same overall composition but differ in the distribution of water molecules.

### 5.5 Linkage Isomerism

Linkage isomerism occurs when an ambidentate ligand coordinates through different donor atoms.

Example:

$[Co(NH_3)_5(NO_2)]Cl_2$

Possible forms:

- Nitro form: Coordination through nitrogen.
    
- Nitrito form: Coordination through oxygen.
    

### 5.6 Coordination Isomerism

Coordination isomerism occurs when ligands are exchanged between the complex cation and complex anion.

Example:

$[Co(NH_3)_6][Cr(CN)_6]$

and

$[Cr(NH_3)_6][Co(CN)_6]$

### 5.7 Stereoisomerism

Stereoisomers have the same connectivity but different spatial arrangements.

Types:

1. Geometrical isomerism.
    
2. Optical isomerism.
    

### 5.8 Geometrical Isomerism

Geometrical isomerism occurs due to different spatial arrangements of ligands around the central metal ion.

#### Cis and Trans Isomerism

**Cis isomer:**

Similar ligands are adjacent to each other.

**Trans isomer:**

Similar ligands are opposite to each other.

Example:

$[Pt(NH_3)_2Cl_2]$

<ins>Square planar geometry</ins>

- Cisplatin: Two $NH_3$ ligands are adjacent.
    
- Transplatin: Two $NH_3$ ligands are opposite.
    

Other examples:

$[Co(NH_3)_4Cl_2]^+$

This octahedral complex has cis and trans forms.

### 5.9 Geometrical Isomerism in Octahedral Complexes

#### Type 1: $MA_4B_2$

Two geometrical isomers are possible:

- Cis.
    
- Trans.
    

Example:

$[Co(NH_3)_4Cl_2]^+$

#### Type 2: $MA_3B_3$

Two geometrical isomers are possible:

- Facial (fac).
    
- Meridional (mer).
    

Example:

$[Co(NH_3)_3Cl_3]$

**Facial Isomer:**

Three identical ligands occupy one triangular face of the octahedron. All three identical ligands are mutually cis.

**Meridional Isomer:**

Three identical ligands lie along a meridian plane. Two of the identical ligands are trans.

#### Type 3: $MA_2B_4$

Two geometrical isomers are possible:

- Cis.
    
- Trans.
    

#### Type 4: $M(AA)_2B_2$

Two geometrical arrangements are possible:

- Cis.
    
- Trans.
    

Here, $AA$ represents a bidentate ligand.

The cis form can be optically active.

### 5.10 Optical Isomerism

Optical isomerism occurs when compounds exist as non-superimposable mirror images.

These mirror-image forms are called enantiomers.

They rotate plane-polarised light in opposite directions.

- Dextrorotatory: Rotates light clockwise.
    
- Laevorotatory: Rotates light anticlockwise.
    

#### Examples

$[Co(en)_3]^{3+}$

This complex exists as two optical isomers.

$[Cr(ox)_3]^{3-}$

This complex also exhibits optical isomerism.

#### Important Points

- Optical isomers are non-superimposable mirror images.
    
- They have identical physical properties in an achiral environment except for their optical rotation.
    
- A racemic mixture contains equal amounts of both enantiomers and is optically inactive.
