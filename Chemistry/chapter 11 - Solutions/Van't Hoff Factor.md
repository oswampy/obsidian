
$\boxed{i=\frac{\text{Observed colligative property}}{\text{Calculated colligative property}}}$

It can also be written as:

$\boxed{i=\frac{\text{Normal molar mass}}{\text{Observed molar mass}}}$

---

# Modified Colligative Property Equations

For abnormal behaviour:

$\boxed{\frac{\Delta p}{p^0}=ix_2}$

$\boxed{\Delta T_b=iK_bm}$

$\boxed{\Delta T_f=iK_fm}$

$\boxed{\pi=iCRT}$

These are **extremely important for JEE**.

---

# When $i>1$

If the solute **dissociates**, the number of particles increases.

Therefore:

$\boxed{i>1}$

Example:

$NaCl\rightarrow Na^++Cl^-$

One particle becomes two.

---

# When $i<1$

If the solute **associates**, the number of particles decreases.

Therefore:

$\boxed{i<1}$

Example:

$2CH_3COOH\rightleftharpoons(CH_3COOH)_2$

Two particles combine to form one particle.

---

# When $i=1$

No association or dissociation occurs.

Therefore:

$\boxed{i=1}$

Example:

Glucose in dilute aqueous solution.

---

# Degree of Dissociation

Suppose one mole of electrolyte dissociates into $\nu$ particles.

If degree of dissociation is $\alpha$:

Initial particles:

$1$

After dissociation:

$1-\alpha+\nu\alpha$

Therefore:

$i=1-\alpha+\nu\alpha$

Hence:

$\boxed{i=1+\alpha(\nu-1)}$

Therefore:

$\boxed{\alpha=\frac{i-1}{\nu-1}}$

---

# Example: NaCl

$NaCl\rightarrow Na^++Cl^-$

Here:

$\nu=2$

Therefore:

$i=1+\alpha$

If complete dissociation occurs:

$\alpha=1$

so:

$i=2$

---

# Example: $CaCl_2$

$CaCl_2\rightarrow Ca^{2+}+2Cl^-$

Total particles:

$\nu=3$

Therefore:

$\boxed{i=1+2\alpha}$

For complete dissociation:

$\alpha=1$

so:

$i=3$

---

# Degree of Association

Suppose $n$ molecules associate to form one molecule:

$nA\rightleftharpoons A_n$

If degree of association is $\alpha$:

$i=1-\alpha+\frac{\alpha}{n}$

Therefore:

$\boxed{i=1-\alpha\left(1-\frac1n\right)}$

or:

$\boxed{i=1-\frac{(n-1)\alpha}{n}}$

---

# Dimerisation

A particularly important case is dimerisation:

$2A\rightleftharpoons A_2$

Here:

$n=2$

Therefore:

$\boxed{i=1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}$

Hence:

$\boxed{\alpha=2(1-i)}$

---

# Association and Molar Mass

Since:

$i=\frac{M_{normal}}{M_{observed}}$

if association occurs:

$i<1$

Therefore:

$M_{observed}>M_{normal}$

So the observed molar mass becomes **larger**.

---

# Dissociation and Molar Mass

If dissociation occurs:

$i>1$

Therefore:

$M_{observed}<M_{normal}$

So the observed molar mass becomes **smaller**.

# JEE Shortcut: Particle Counting

Whenever you see a colligative-property question involving an electrolyte, first ask:

**How many particles are produced?**

For example:

$NaCl\rightarrow2\text{ particles}$

$CaCl_2\rightarrow3\text{ particles}$

$AlCl_3\rightarrow4\text{ particles}$

$Na_2SO_4\rightarrow3\text{ particles}$

If complete dissociation occurs:

$i=\text{number of particles produced}$

---

# Important JEE Concept: Complete vs Partial Dissociation

Do not automatically use the number of ions as $i$.

For example:

$CaCl_2\rightleftharpoons Ca^{2+}+2Cl^-$

If only $50%$ dissociates:

$\alpha=0.5$

Then:

$i=1+2(0.5)$

$i=2$

not $3$.

---

# Molar Mass from Colligative Properties

For elevation of boiling point:

$\Delta T_b=iK_bm$

Substitute:

$m=\frac{1000w_2}{M_2w_1}$

Therefore:

$\Delta T_b=iK_b\frac{1000w_2}{M_2w_1}$

Thus:

$\boxed{M_2=\frac{iK_b1000w_2}{\Delta T_bw_1}}$

---

# Molar Mass from Depression in Freezing Point

$\Delta T_f=iK_fm$

Therefore:

$\boxed{M_2=\frac{iK_f1000w_2}{\Delta T_fw_1}}$

---

# Molar Mass from Osmotic Pressure

$\pi=i\frac{w_2}{M_2V}RT$

Therefore:

$\boxed{M_2=\frac{iw_2RT}{\pi V}}$

---

# Molar Mass from Relative Lowering of Vapour Pressure

$\frac{\Delta p}{p^0}=ix_2$

For a dilute solution:

$\frac{\Delta p}{p^0}=i\frac{n_2}{n_1}$

Therefore:

$\frac{\Delta p}{p^0}=i\frac{w_2/M_2}{w_1/M_1}$

Hence:

$\boxed{M_2=\frac{iw_2M_1p^0}{w_1\Delta p}}$
