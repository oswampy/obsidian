

### 11.1 Nuclear Fission

Nuclear fission is the process in which a heavy nucleus splits into two or more lighter nuclei, releasing a large amount of energy.

Example:

$^{235}_{92}U+^1_0n\rightarrow^{141}_{56}Ba+^{92}_{36}Kr+3^1_0n+Q$

The exact products may vary.

#### Important Features

- A heavy nucleus absorbs a neutron.
    
- It becomes unstable.
    
- It splits into smaller nuclei.
    
- Additional neutrons are released.
    
- A large amount of energy is released.
    

### 11.2 Chain Reaction

A chain reaction occurs when neutrons produced in one fission event cause further fission events.

**Controlled chain reaction**

The reaction is maintained at a controlled rate.

**Uncontrolled chain reaction**

The number of fission events increases rapidly.

### 11.3 Nuclear Reactor

A nuclear reactor is a system in which a controlled nuclear fission chain reaction is maintained to produce useful energy.

Main components:

- Nuclear fuel: undergoes fission.
    
- Moderator: slows down neutrons.
    
- Control rods: absorb excess neutrons.
    
- Coolant: transfers heat.
    
- Shielding: reduces radiation exposure.
    

### 11.4 Nuclear Fusion

Nuclear fusion is the process in which two light nuclei combine to form a heavier nucleus, releasing energy.

Example:

$^2_1H+^3_1H\rightarrow^4_2He+^1_0n+17.6\ MeV$

#### Important Features

- Requires very high temperatures to overcome electrostatic repulsion.
    
- Releases a large amount of energy.
    
- Occurs naturally in stars.
    
- Light nuclei generally undergo fusion.
    

### 11.5 Fission vs Fusion

|Property|Fission|Fusion|
|---|---|---|
|Process|Heavy nucleus splits|Light nuclei combine|
|Starting nuclei|Heavy|Light|
|Products|Medium-sized nuclei|Heavier nucleus|
|Energy release|Large|Large|
|Conditions|Neutron-induced or other suitable conditions|Very high temperature|
|Natural occurrence|Some radioactive nuclei|Stars|
