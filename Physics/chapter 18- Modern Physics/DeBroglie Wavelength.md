## 4. Matter Waves — de Broglie Hypothesis

### 4.1 Wave Nature of Matter

According to de Broglie, every moving material particle has an associated wave.

These waves are called **matter waves** or de Broglie waves.

The wavelength is:

$\lambda=\dfrac{h}{p}$

For a non-relativistic particle:

$\lambda=\dfrac{h}{mv}$

### 4.2 de Broglie Wavelength of an Electron Accelerated Through Potential V

When an electron is accelerated through a potential difference $V$, the electrical energy gained becomes kinetic energy.

$eV=\dfrac{1}{2}m_ev^2$

Therefore:

$p=\sqrt{2m_eeV}$

Hence:

$\lambda=\dfrac{h}{\sqrt{2m_eeV}}$

For an electron:

$\lambda=\dfrac{12.27}{\sqrt{V}}\ \mathring A$

Here, $V$ is in volts.

### 4.3 de Broglie Wavelength of a Charged Particle

For a particle of charge $q$ and mass $m$ accelerated through potential difference $V$:

$\lambda=\dfrac{h}{\sqrt{2mqV}}$

This expression assumes the particle starts approximately from rest and behaves non-relativistically.

### 4.4 de Broglie Wavelength in Terms of Kinetic Energy

If kinetic energy is $K$:

$\lambda=\dfrac{h}{\sqrt{2mK}}$

### 4.5 de Broglie Wavelength in Terms of Temperature

For a particle having kinetic energy approximately equal to $\dfrac{3}{2}k_BT$:

$\lambda=\dfrac{h}{\sqrt{3mk_BT}}$

This is a characteristic wavelength based on the average translational kinetic energy of a classical ideal-gas particle.

### 4.6 Important Comparisons

**For two particles having the same momentum:**

$\lambda_1=\lambda_2$

**For two particles having the same kinetic energy:**

$\dfrac{\lambda_1}{\lambda_2}=\sqrt{\dfrac{m_2}{m_1}}$

**For two particles having the same velocity:**

$\dfrac{\lambda_1}{\lambda_2}=\dfrac{m_2}{m_1}$

**For the same charged particle accelerated through different potentials:**

$\lambda\propto\dfrac{1}{\sqrt V}$

### 4.7 Davisson–Germer Experiment

The Davisson–Germer experiment experimentally confirmed the wave nature of electrons.

- Electrons were accelerated through a potential difference.
    
- They were directed towards a nickel crystal.
    
- The scattered electrons showed diffraction patterns.
    
- The experimentally observed wavelength agreed with the de Broglie wavelength.
    

This established the wave-particle duality of matter.

### 4.8 Important JEE Concepts

**Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle**

It is impossible to simultaneously determine the exact position and momentum of a particle.

$\Delta x\Delta p\geq\dfrac{\hbar}{2}$

Where:

$\hbar=\dfrac{h}{2\pi}$

Since $p=mv$ for a non-relativistic particle:

$\Delta x\Delta v\geq\dfrac{\hbar}{2m}$

**Wave-particle duality**

- Radiation shows wave and particle behaviour.
    
- Matter also shows wave and particle behaviour.
    
- The observed behaviour depends on the experimental situation.
